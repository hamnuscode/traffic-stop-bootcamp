Four modules · Watch in order

The program, module by module.

Each module runs inside a single class period. Watch straight through the first time, then use the discussion prompts to work back through the moments that matter most for your group.

Module 01

The First Five Minutes

The opening moments of a stop set its tone. This module covers what happens from the instant the lights appear to the first words exchanged: where to pull over, how to position the car, what to do with your hands, and why announcing every movement matters more than anything you say.

  • Choosing a safe, visible place to stop
  • Engine, windows, and interior lighting after dark
  • Hand placement and narrating each movement
  • Passengers: what they should and shouldn't do

Module 02

Communication Skills

What you say is only part of it. This module works on tone, pace, and word choice — answering questions honestly without volunteering trouble, staying level when a question feels unfair, and keeping the exchange on the business of the stop.

  • Answering directly without escalating
  • Tone, volume, and pace under pressure
  • Phrases that keep the exchange steady
  • Staying respectful while holding your ground

Module 03

De-Escalation Lite

Tension has tells, and most of them show up before anyone raises their voice. This module teaches how to read those early signals and bring the temperature down with posture, pace, and stillness — practical technique, not theory.

  • Early signals that a stop is tightening
  • Body language that lowers the temperature
  • Managing your own adrenaline in the moment
  • When to say less and simply comply

Module 04

Dealing with Consent and Final Steps

The last module covers the part most people are least sure about: what consent to a search actually means, how to decline one calmly and clearly, and how a stop properly ends — including what to write down afterwards if something about it needs to be raised later.

  • What consent to a search does and doesn't cover
  • Declining clearly, calmly, and once
  • Asking whether you're free to go
  • Recording details and filing a complaint afterwards
